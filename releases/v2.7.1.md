OCBM 2.7.1: an update package for adapters on OCBM 1.0.1 to 2.7.0, once their install is final. A fix for the Bluetooth firmware load at power-up, and shorter waits in the first connect.

- **Fixed: Bluetooth attached without its firmware.** At power-up the adapter runs the chip maker's loader to send the Bluetooth chip its firmware. Every release until now ran it with no time limit and ignored its result, so when the load failed the adapter attached Bluetooth to a chip with no firmware; on a cold start on the bench (22 Nov) the adapter then went silent until it was powered off and on. The loader now has 10 s, a load that fails is run once more, and after two failures the adapter attaches nothing and says why in its log; Carlink's connect then starts Bluetooth afresh, with a load of its own. A load also follows each Bluetooth reset, before the next attach. Once a load has succeeded since the adapter started, a later one that fails does not stop the attach: the chip still runs that firmware.
- **The first call lasts 5.12 s.** When Carlink arrives and the adapter calls the paired phones, the first call, to the first phone in the order set in Carlink's Phones page, now lasts 5.12 s, the Bluetooth controller's full call, instead of 3.75 s, because the phone took more than 2 s to answer on 3 of 7 cold starts. The other phones in that first round are still called for 3.75 s each, in the same order, and later rounds for 5.12 s. With one paired phone every call already lasted 5.12 s.
- **A Bluetooth connect that stalls is cut at 1.5 s.** After the phone answered and named its CarPlay channel, the adapter waited up to 8 s for that channel to open, then went on to the next phone. It now waits 1.5 s, drops the link and calls the same phone once more. A pairing prompt on the phone still holds the wait, as before.
- **The same phone called again.** When the adapter calls a phone again after a reset or a cut and that call fails because the Bluetooth controller still holds a link to the phone, the adapter now waits up to 1.5 s for the link to clear and calls the same phone again, at most twice, instead of going on to the next phone. On the truck on 21 Nov this case handed the session to the other phone.
- **Shorter steps at power-up.** When Carlink arrives while the adapter is still loading its radios, the Bluetooth service starts at once and waits for the radio, instead of starting after it; the radio load no longer sets the Bluetooth name and the call-audio (SCO) settings, which the Bluetooth service's own start resets anyway (it writes the name itself); and the adapter looks every 0.05 s, not every 0.2 s, for the radio load to let the radio go. After each Bluetooth attach the adapter looks every 0.1 s, not every 0.5 s, for the chip to answer.
- **The screen asked for at once.** Some iPhones set up their screen stream, tear it down again a few tens of ms later with no frame, then set it up again (an iPhone 7 on the bench, on nearly every connect). The adapter now asks such a phone for its screen again at once instead of after 500 ms: on the bench the first frame then came about 0.1 s after the phone's second screen set-up, against about 1.9 s with the wait, in each of 40 wakes. A phone that keeps its screen stream is not affected.
- **The `[radio-boot]` line** gains `btfw_tries`: how many times the firmware loader ran in that bring-up. docs/ops/02_TESTING.md lists the keys.
- **Switches for the bench.** Each new default above can be turned back to 2.7.0's behaviour by a file on the adapter, /etc/ocbm/bench_<name> (or /tmp/bench_<name>, which a restart clears) reading `off`; a few more switches stay off. docs/ops/02_TESTING.md, "Round 4 switches", lists them. Nothing in OCBM or in a release build of Carlink writes these files.

**From 2.7.0 and earlier, for adapters on 2.6.5 or older** (they update straight to 2.7.1):

- **The radios loaded earlier, and together** (2.7.0). After power-up the adapter starts loading its radios as soon as its pins are set, and on adapters with the NXP IW416 radio it loads the Bluetooth firmware and the Wi-Fi driver side by side, calling the phone about 2 s sooner. On the bench the first CarPlay frame came 13.8 to 16.9 s after power-on when the iPhone answered the adapter's first call. To turn the side-by-side load off, create the file /script/radio_together_off on the adapter.
- **Sooner wakes** (2.6.5). Between sessions the adapter keeps Bluetooth powered but invisible, and starts its Wi-Fi access point and its CarPlay receiver earlier. On the bench a wake reached the picture in 5.2 to 6.4 s with the iPhone in use, where 2.6.1 took 8.2 to 9.4 s.
- **A `[timeline]` line for each connect** (2.6.1). At the first video frame of a wireless connect the adapter logs the time of each step since Carlink arrived. Carlink keeps the line with its own times while Record drive logs is on.
- **A change of phone in about 5 s** (2.6.0). When an iPhone other than the last session's connects, it reaches the CarPlay picture about 5 s after it joins the adapter's Wi-Fi, instead of 40-90 s.

Releases v2.7.0, v2.6.5, v2.6.1 and v2.6.0 say more. Adapters on 2.0.0 or older also get 2.5.0's faster wireless connects and boot preload (release v2.5.0), and adapters on 1.0.x get 2.0.0's changes too: video from the iPhone while parked, the car's GPS and status for the iPhone, and one name for the adapter's Wi-Fi and Bluetooth (release v2.0.0).

The package changes 17 files (btd, carplayd, iap2d, iap_role_switch, ocbmd and 12 scripts) plus etc/ocbm/retired.list, counted against every published version from 1.0.1 to 2.7.0. Against 2.7.0 it changes btd, carplayd and three scripts (ocbm_boot.sh, radio_hal.sh, session_supervisor.sh). It needs about <NEED_KB> KB of flash while it runs, so it does not fit a trial: an adapter still in its trial finishes its install first (in Carlink: Settings › Adapter and about › Remove stock CarPlay), then takes the update. Updates from 1.0.1 (FROM_MIN 1.0.1). Built from enigy/ocbm <COMMIT> ("2.7.1 ocbm <COMMIT>"); iap2d and iap_role_switch, whose programs are unchanged, are 2.0.0's builds, as in 2.7.0.

Install it from Carlink <CARLINK> (Settings > Adapter and about), or put ocbm-update-2.7.1.tgz and its .sha256 on a USB stick and start the adapter with it in. The installer image for a stock adapter is on release installer-2.7.1.

Going back to stock from a finished adapter wipes its paired phones: forget the adapter (CarLink-xxxx) on each phone first, and pair them again afterwards.

No update is guaranteed to succeed. The update runs as a trial and falls back to the version it replaced if its self-test fails, the adapter never answers Carlink, or its Bluetooth never comes back.
