OCBM 2.6.0: an update package for adapters on OCBM 1.0.1 to 2.5.0, once their install is final. A change of phone in about 5 s.

- **A change of phone in about 5 s.** When an iPhone other than the last session's connects, it now reaches the CarPlay picture about 5 s after it joins the adapter's Wi-Fi, instead of 40-90 s. Measured on a bench adapter with two iPhones, on the 2.6.0 candidate:
  - a change of phone, from the phone joining the adapter's Wi-Fi to the CarPlay picture: 4.6 s from phone B to phone A (44 s before), and 5.7 s from A to B (91 s before);
  - the same phone back: about 6 s;
  - a switch from Carlink's Phones page: about 9 s from the tap;
  - a cold connect after an adapter restart, from Carlink's USB OK to the CarPlay picture: 12.6 s (2.5.0: 12.1 s).
- **Why it took so long.** A phone that knows the adapter's Wi-Fi joins it before the Bluetooth handoff names it, so the adapter turned it away as not the last session's phone until it happened to look again. The adapter now names a paired phone as its Bluetooth session starts, while no CarPlay session is up, and looks for it again as soon as the name changes. As before, the phone a Carlink phone switch left can't take the session back.
- **Shorter connect steps.** Bluetooth is powered up once per connect, not cycled a second time. The adapter starts its radios as soon as Carlink arrives, not at its next 1 s check, and waits for any teardown of the last session to end first. After the Wi-Fi handoff it looks for the phone's screen and event connections every 10 ms, not every 0.1-0.5 s, and the address check before a new Wi-Fi lease takes 250 ms, not 2 s.
- **A stalled Bluetooth answer is cut at 0.7 s.** When a phone answers the adapter's page but opens no Bluetooth service channel within 0.7 s, the adapter cuts that try and pages the phone again at once. A cut counts at most once a round toward a forgotten pairing, so a paired phone that wasn't ready yet (after a power cut, say) is not taken for one that forgot the adapter.
- **The MFi certificate is read once per boot.** The adapter reads it from its authentication chip once, not at each session (on the bench, each session's first read was refused). It keeps the copy only when it checks out as one DER certificate, and drops it when an iPhone reports a failed authentication (0xAA04), so the next session reads the chip again.
- **The Wi-Fi password.** An adapter that goes back to stock in its install trial keeps the Wi-Fi password OCBM gave it for the next install, so each phone that knew it joins at once instead of retrying for about 6 s at its first connect. A stock adapter's own password (/etc/wifi_password) is now taken over whole: before, the adapter's tr cut it to a few letters, and a random password usually replaced it. Any printable password, spaces included, is written and handed to the phone exactly as it was read.
- **Bench hooks.** Test hooks for the bench (docs/ops/02_TESTING.md) do nothing unless their files in /tmp are set. Nothing in OCBM or in a release build of Carlink sets them, and a restart clears them.
- The adapter's [startup] log line also gives its uptime when Carlink arrived, the boot preload's state then, and, since Carlink came, the pages with no answer, the resets and the Wi-Fi password mismatches. The Wi-Fi log says where the password came from, never the password. The protocol is unchanged.

**From 2.5.0, for adapters on 2.0.0 or older** (they update straight to 2.6.0):

- **Faster wireless connects.** A cold connect after an adapter restart, from Carlink's USB OK to the CarPlay picture, took a median 12.1 s on the bench, against 22.5 s on 2.0.0, and Bluetooth is up 1.5 s after the adapter sees the car, against 11.3 s.
- **The boot preload.** While the car starts, the adapter loads its radio drivers and attaches Bluetooth, with nothing a phone can see (no Wi-Fi network, and Bluetooth powered down), so the first connect skips that work. It is on by default; to turn it off, create /script/radio_preload_off. Release v2.5.0 says more.
- **Bluetooth and the Wi-Fi network come up side by side.** The adapter tries each phone briefly first (about 4 s each), in the order set in Carlink's Phones page.
- If the phone drops its screen stream, the adapter asks for the picture again after 0.5 s, and once more at 3 s.

Adapters on 1.0.x also get 2.0.0's changes: video from the iPhone while parked (route A, iOS 26 and later, with Carlink 3.00 or later), the car's GPS and status for the iPhone, a stuck wireless session that ends by itself, and one name for the adapter's Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. Release v2.0.0 lists them, and what it retired.

The package changes 17 files (btd, carplayd, iap2d, iap_role_switch, ocbmd and 12 scripts) plus etc/ocbm/retired.list, counted against every published version from 1.0.1 to 2.5.0. Against 2.5.0 it changes btd, carplayd, ocbmd and five scripts (ocbm_boot.sh, ocbm_update.sh, radio_ap_up.sh, radio_hal.sh, session_supervisor.sh). It needs about <NEED_KB> KB of flash while it runs, so it does not fit a trial: an adapter still in its trial finishes its install first (in Carlink: Settings › Adapter and about › Remove stock CarPlay), then takes the update. Updates from 1.0.1 (FROM_MIN 1.0.1). Built from enigy/ocbm <COMMIT> ("2.6.0 ocbm <COMMIT>"); iap2d and iap_role_switch, whose programs are unchanged, are 2.0.0's builds, as in 2.5.0.

Install it from Carlink 3.20 (Settings > Adapter and about), or put ocbm-update-2.6.0.tgz and its .sha256 on a USB stick and start the adapter with it in. The installer image for a stock adapter is on release installer-2.6.0.

Going back to stock from a finished adapter wipes its paired phones: forget the adapter (CarLink-xxxx) on each phone first, and pair them again afterwards.

No update is guaranteed to succeed. The update runs as a trial and falls back to the version it replaced if its self-test fails, the adapter never answers Carlink, or its Bluetooth never comes back.
