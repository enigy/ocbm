OCBM 2.5.0: an update package for adapters on OCBM 1.0.1 to 2.0.0, once their install is final. Faster wireless connects.

- **Faster wireless connects.** Measured on a bench adapter with one iPhone, listed first and in range, and Carlink 3.10:
  - a cold connect after an adapter restart, from Carlink's USB OK to the CarPlay picture: a median 12.1 s over 3 runs, against 22.5 s (1 run) on 2.0.0 measured the same way;
  - a connect when the display wakes, with the adapter still powered, from display on to the CarPlay picture: a median 11.3 s over 5 runs;
  - Bluetooth is up 1.5 s after the adapter sees the car, against 11.3 s.
- **The boot preload.** While the car starts, the adapter loads its radio drivers and attaches Bluetooth, with nothing a phone can see (no Wi-Fi network, and Bluetooth powered down), so the first connect skips that work. It is on by default; to turn it off, create /script/radio_preload_off. If two preloads go unfinished with none finishing between (a hang, or power lost inside its 7 s), the adapter turns it off by itself and writes why into that file; delete the file to turn it back on. On an adapter in its install or update trial, the preload waits for the start-up self-test to pass (at most 120 s).
- **Bluetooth and the Wi-Fi network come up side by side.** The adapter tries each phone briefly first (about 4 s each), in the order set in Carlink's Phones page. A phone whose page timed out is tried last in the next round. For two minutes from when the adapter starts its radios, rounds are 5 s apart, so a phone that comes back then is found within about 5-7 s, plus about 5 s for each phone listed ahead of it that is still away; after that the gap grows from 10 s to 60 s.
- A phone that answers and then drops the link straight after another phone's page timed out is no longer read as having forgotten the pairing.
- If the phone drops its screen stream, the adapter asks for the picture again after 0.5 s, and once more at 3 s.
- The adapter's log times each connect's stages in one [startup] line, and logs every hello from Carlink with the adapter's uptime. The protocol gains one MGMT_INFO field, preload; nothing else changes.

**From 2.0.0, for adapters on 1.0.x** (they update straight to 2.5.0):

- **Video from the iPhone, parked (route A).** The adapter offers AirPlay video inside the CarPlay session (iOS 26 and later). With Carlink 3.00 or later, a video you send from the iPhone (Safari, for example) plays full screen on the car's screen while the car is parked. It is fetched through the iPhone's own network, never the car's. The iPhone decides what it offers; FairPlay-protected video (the TV app's) stays on the iPhone.
- **The car's GPS and status for the iPhone**, when Carlink pushes them (Settings › Vehicle).
- **A stuck wireless session ends by itself.** A Bluetooth session whose iPhone never reaches CarPlay ends 90 s in, and the phones are called again, so CarPlay comes back once the iPhone's Wi-Fi does.
- **One name.** The adapter's Wi-Fi has the same name as its Bluetooth, CarLink-<4 hex>.
- Retired: the AirPlay video probe's switch file (/etc/ocbm/airplay_probe) is ignored, and protocol command 0x14 (the screen borrow) is gone. docs/carplay/01_OCBM_PROTOCOL.md describes route A's wire.

The package changes 17 files (btd, carplayd, iap2d, iap_role_switch, ocbmd and 12 scripts) plus etc/ocbm/retired.list, counted against every published version from 1.0.1 to 2.0.0. It needs about 3141 KB of flash while it runs, so it does not fit a trial: an adapter still in its trial finishes its install first (in Carlink, Remove stock firmware), then takes the update. Updates from 1.0.1 (FROM_MIN 1.0.1). Built from enigy/ocbm 92ab7a5 ("2.5.0 ocbm 92ab7a5"); iap2d and iap_role_switch, whose sources are unchanged, are 2.0.0's builds.

Install it from Carlink 3.10 (Settings > Adapter and about), or put ocbm-update-2.5.0.tgz and its .sha256 on a USB stick and start the adapter with it in. The installer image for a stock adapter is on release installer-2.5.0.

Going back to stock from a finished adapter wipes its paired phones: forget the adapter (CarLink-xxxx) on each phone first, and pair them again afterwards.

No update is guaranteed to succeed. The update runs as a trial and falls back to the version it replaced if its self-test fails, the adapter never answers Carlink, or its Bluetooth never comes back.
