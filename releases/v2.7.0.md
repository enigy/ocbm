OCBM 2.7.0: an update package for adapters on OCBM 1.0.1 to 2.6.5, once their install is final. The adapter is ready sooner after it gets power, which is the start a car sees whenever it has cut the adapter's USB power while parked. On the bench, with Carlink waiting as the adapter powered up, the first CarPlay frame came 13.8 to 16.9 s after power-on when the iPhone answered the adapter's first call (9 starts). The same build with the old loading order took 16.2 and 17.4 s (2 starts).

- **Both radios loaded together.** After power-up the adapter sends the Bluetooth chip its firmware and loads the Wi-Fi driver side by side, instead of one after the other, and calls the phone about 2 s sooner. This is for adapters with the NXP IW416 radio whose factory start-up script loads Bluetooth without waiting for Wi-Fi; any other adapter keeps the old order. If the load fails, the adapter goes back to the old order in the same start, and after two failed starts in a row it stays on the old order until the next update. To turn it off, create the file /script/radio_together_off on the adapter.
- **The radios loaded earlier in the start.** The load begins as soon as the adapter's pins are set, not at the end of its start-up script. The file /script/radio_preload_late gives the old start; installs and updates keep that file.
- **The radio chip's description kept.** The adapter keeps what it detected about its radio chip for later starts, and detects afresh when the chip or its scripts change.
- **Fixed: the start-up guards during an update's trial.** While an update was in its trial, every start cleared the guard that turns the early radio load off after repeated failed starts, so it could not act during a trial. The guards are now cleared once per update.
- **A `[radio-boot]` line.** One log line per start: the loading order, when each step ended, the tries and the result. It names no phone. docs/ops/02_TESTING.md lists the keys.
- **Carried but off: the direct handover.** A further step passes the Bluetooth the adapter started at power-up straight to the session, without restarting it. On the bench it saved about 1.5 s, but 2 of its 6 starts had a slow first connect, so it is off. The file /script/radio_handover_on turns it on.

**From 2.6.5 and earlier, for adapters on 2.6.1 or older** (they update straight to 2.7.0):

- **Sooner wakes** (2.6.5). Between sessions the adapter keeps Bluetooth powered but invisible, and starts its Wi-Fi access point and its CarPlay receiver earlier. On the bench a wake reached the picture in 5.2 to 6.4 s with the iPhone in use, where 2.6.1 took 8.2 to 9.4 s.
- **A `[timeline]` line for each connect** (2.6.1). At the first video frame of a wireless connect the adapter logs the time of each step since Carlink arrived. Carlink keeps the line with its own times while Record drive logs is on.
- **A change of phone in about 5 s** (2.6.0). When an iPhone other than the last session's connects, it reaches the CarPlay picture about 5 s after it joins the adapter's Wi-Fi, instead of 40-90 s.
- **The Wi-Fi password** (2.6.0). An adapter that goes back to stock in its install trial keeps the Wi-Fi password OCBM gave it for the next install, and an install from stock takes over the adapter's own password whole.

Releases v2.6.5, v2.6.1 and v2.6.0 say more. Adapters on 2.0.0 or older also get 2.5.0's faster wireless connects and boot preload (release v2.5.0), and adapters on 1.0.x get 2.0.0's changes too: video from the iPhone while parked, the car's GPS and status for the iPhone, and one name for the adapter's Wi-Fi and Bluetooth (release v2.0.0).

The package changes 17 files (btd, carplayd, iap2d, iap_role_switch, ocbmd and 12 scripts) plus etc/ocbm/retired.list, counted against every published version from 1.0.1 to 2.6.5. Against 2.6.5 it changes btd and eight scripts (ocbm_boot.sh, ocbm_dispatch.sh, ocbm_revert.sh, ocbm_update.sh, radio_detect.sh, radio_hal.sh, session_supervisor.sh, stock_restore_box.sh). It needs about 3331 KB of flash while it runs, so it does not fit a trial: an adapter still in its trial finishes its install first (in Carlink: Settings › Adapter and about › Remove stock CarPlay), then takes the update. Updates from 1.0.1 (FROM_MIN 1.0.1). Built from enigy/ocbm f2486c9 ("2.7.0 ocbm f2486c9"); iap2d and iap_role_switch, whose programs are unchanged, are 2.0.0's builds, as in 2.6.5.

Install it from Carlink 3.30 (Settings > Adapter and about), or put ocbm-update-2.7.0.tgz and its .sha256 on a USB stick and start the adapter with it in. The installer image for a stock adapter is on release installer-2.7.0.

Going back to stock from a finished adapter wipes its paired phones: forget the adapter (CarLink-xxxx) on each phone first, and pair them again afterwards.

No update is guaranteed to succeed. The update runs as a trial and falls back to the version it replaced if its self-test fails, the adapter never answers Carlink, or its Bluetooth never comes back.
