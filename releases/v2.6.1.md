OCBM 2.6.1: an update package for adapters on OCBM 1.0.1 to 2.6.0, once their install is final. A measuring release: the adapter now times each step of a connect.

- **A `[timeline]` line for each connect.** At the first video frame of a wireless connect the adapter logs one line, right after `[startup]`, with the time of each step since Carlink arrived, in ms: the radios starting, Bluetooth powered, the page and the phone's answer, the Bluetooth authentication, the Wi-Fi details reply, the phone joining the adapter's Wi-Fi and getting its address, the CarPlay receiver finding and dialing the phone, pair-verify, the AirPlay authentication, RECORD and the first frame. Carlink 3.21 keeps the line with its own times for each connect, when the adapter's log is on (Record drive logs).
- **The chip's own times.** Each certificate read and each signature by the adapter's authentication chip logs how long the chip took. On the bench a signature took 1.64 to 1.65 s every time, and a certificate read 0.11 to 0.17 s.
- **The line names no phone.** It holds fixed step names with numbers, or `-` for a step that didn't happen: no phone's name, address or network name, and at most 794 bytes. The phone joining the Wi-Fi is read from the adapter's own log files by a watch that runs only while a connect starts, and keeps nothing of the phone's address but the last two octets in one log line.
- **Nothing else changes.** No timing, order, retry or protocol byte of 2.6.0 is different. docs/ops/02_TESTING.md lists the line's keys and where each is stamped.

**From 2.6.0, for adapters on 2.5.0 or older** (they update straight to 2.6.1):

- **A change of phone in about 5 s.** When an iPhone other than the last session's connects, it reaches the CarPlay picture about 5 s after it joins the adapter's Wi-Fi, instead of 40-90 s.
- **Shorter connect steps.** Bluetooth is powered up once per connect, the adapter starts its radios as soon as Carlink arrives, and a Bluetooth answer that stalls is cut at 0.7 s and paged again at once.
- **The Wi-Fi password.** An adapter that goes back to stock in its install trial keeps the Wi-Fi password OCBM gave it for the next install, and an install from stock takes over the adapter's own password whole.

Release v2.6.0 says more. Adapters on 2.0.0 or older also get 2.5.0's faster wireless connects and boot preload (release v2.5.0), and adapters on 1.0.x get 2.0.0's changes too: video from the iPhone while parked, the car's GPS and status for the iPhone, and one name for the adapter's Wi-Fi and Bluetooth (release v2.0.0).

The package changes 17 files (btd, carplayd, iap2d, iap_role_switch, ocbmd and 12 scripts) plus etc/ocbm/retired.list, counted against every published version from 1.0.1 to 2.6.0. Against 2.6.0 it changes btd, carplayd, ocbmd and three scripts (radio_ap_up.sh, radio_hal.sh, session_supervisor.sh). It needs about 3208 KB of flash while it runs, so it does not fit a trial: an adapter still in its trial finishes its install first (in Carlink: Settings › Adapter and about › Remove stock CarPlay), then takes the update. Updates from 1.0.1 (FROM_MIN 1.0.1). Built from enigy/ocbm 3d289a5 ("2.6.1 ocbm 3d289a5"); iap2d and iap_role_switch, whose programs are unchanged, are 2.0.0's builds, as in 2.6.0.

Install it from Carlink 3.21 (Settings > Adapter and about), or put ocbm-update-2.6.1.tgz and its .sha256 on a USB stick and start the adapter with it in. The installer image for a stock adapter is on release installer-2.6.1.

Going back to stock from a finished adapter wipes its paired phones: forget the adapter (CarLink-xxxx) on each phone first, and pair them again afterwards.

No update is guaranteed to succeed. The update runs as a trial and falls back to the version it replaced if its self-test fails, the adapter never answers Carlink, or its Bluetooth never comes back.
